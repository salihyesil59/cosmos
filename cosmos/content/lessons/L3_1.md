## Describing a cosmic fluid

On large scales every component of the universe behaves like a smooth fluid with
an energy density $\rho c^2$ and a pressure $p$. Their ratio is the
[[equation-of-state|equation of state]] parameter

$$w = \frac{p}{\rho c^2}$$

Three values are fundamental:

| Component | $w$ | Pressure | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[matter|Matter]] ("dust") | $0$ | negligible | atoms, dark matter, galaxies |
| [[radiation|Radiation]] | $1/3$ | positive | photons, neutrinos in the early universe |
| [[cosmological-constant|Cosmological constant]] | $-1$ | negative | vacuum energy, dark energy |

:::note Why can matter be pressureless?
The pressure of a gas comes from the random motion of its particles. For
galaxies, stars and cold dark matter, these speeds are tiny compared with the
speed of light, so $p \ll \rho c^2$ and we can set $w = 0$.
:::

## How each component dilutes

Put $p = w\rho c^2$ into the fluid equation from lesson [L2.3](lesson:L2.3) and
solve. For constant $w$:

$$\rho \propto a^{-3(1+w)}$$

- **Matter**, $w = 0$: $\rho_{\mathrm{m}} \propto a^{-3}$. The number of particles
  is fixed and the volume grows as $a^3$.
- **Radiation**, $w = 1/3$: $\rho_{\mathrm{r}} \propto a^{-4}$. The number of
  photons per volume drops as $a^{-3}$, *and* each photon loses energy because its
  wavelength is stretched by $a$.
- **Cosmological constant**, $w = -1$: $\rho_\Lambda = \mathrm{constant}$. It is
  an energy of empty space itself, so more space means more of it.

{{figure:density_evolution}}

:::key Different ingredients dominate at different times
Going back in time ($a \to 0$), radiation grows fastest and must dominate the
early universe. Later, matter takes over. Finally, as matter thins out, the
constant dark energy density wins. Our universe has passed through all three
eras.
:::

## The three eras

Using the Planck 2018 values:

1. **Radiation era**, until matter–radiation equality at $z_{\mathrm{eq}} \approx 3\,400$,
   about 50 000 years after the Big Bang. Setting
   $\Omega_{\mathrm{r},0}a^{-4} = \Omega_{\mathrm{m},0}a^{-3}$ gives
   $a_{\mathrm{eq}} = \Omega_{\mathrm{r},0}/\Omega_{\mathrm{m},0}$.
2. **Matter era**, during which galaxies and large-scale structure grew.
3. **Dark energy era**. Matter and dark energy densities became equal at
   $z \approx 0.3$, about 3.5 billion years ago. The expansion started to
   accelerate even a little earlier, at $z \approx 0.6$.

## Expansion in each era

When one component dominates a flat universe, the Friedmann equation
$\dot{a}^2 \propto \rho a^2 \propto a^{-1-3w}$ has a simple power-law solution:

$$a(t) \propto t^{\,2/(3(1+w))}$$

| Era | $w$ | Scale factor | Hubble parameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Radiation | $1/3$ | $a \propto t^{1/2}$ | $H = 1/(2t)$ |
| Matter | $0$ | $a \propto t^{2/3}$ | $H = 2/(3t)$ |
| Cosmological constant | $-1$ | $a \propto e^{H_\Lambda t}$ | $H = H_\Lambda$ = constant |

In the first two eras the expansion decelerates. With a cosmological constant the
expansion rate stays constant, so the scale factor grows **exponentially**.

:::warning Acceleration needs w < -1/3
From the acceleration equation, $\ddot{a} \propto -(\rho + 3p/c^2) = -\rho(1+3w)$.
Any component with $w < -1/3$ accelerates the expansion. A cosmological constant
($w = -1$) easily satisfies this; normal matter and radiation never do.
:::

:::try S2 Set ΩΛ to zero and then increase it: watch the a(t) curve bend upward once dark energy dominates.

## Summary

- The equation of state $w = p/\rho c^2$ characterises each component.
- Densities dilute as $\rho \propto a^{-3(1+w)}$: matter $a^{-3}$, radiation
  $a^{-4}$, cosmological constant constant.
- The universe was radiation dominated, then matter dominated, and is now dark
  energy dominated.
- $a \propto t^{1/2}$ (radiation), $t^{2/3}$ (matter), $e^{Ht}$ (Λ); acceleration
  requires $w < -1/3$.
