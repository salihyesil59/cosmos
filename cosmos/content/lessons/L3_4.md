## The standard model of cosmology

Putting together everything from Levels 1–3, cosmologists arrived at a model that
fits a huge range of observations. It is called [[lambda-cdm|ΛCDM]]
("Lambda-CDM"):

- **Λ**: a cosmological constant (dark energy with $w = -1$);
- **CDM**: *cold dark matter*, a type of dark matter whose particles moved slowly
  (were "cold") when structures began to form;
- plus ordinary matter (baryons), photons and neutrinos;
- a spatially **flat** geometry;
- an early hot, dense phase (the Big Bang) and tiny initial density fluctuations
  with almost the same strength on all scales.

## The energy budget

{{figure:energy_budget}}

| Component | Density parameter today | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Dark energy (Λ) | $\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.69$ | about 69% |
| Cold dark matter | $\Omega_{\mathrm{c}} \approx 0.26$ | about 26% |
| Ordinary matter (baryons) | $\Omega_{\mathrm{b}} \approx 0.049$ | about 5% |
| Radiation (photons + neutrinos) | $\Omega_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 9 \times 10^{-5}$ | 0.01% |

:::key We understand only 5%
Everything ever seen through a telescope, every star, planet and gas cloud, is
part of the 5% of ordinary matter. The other 95% is dark matter and dark energy,
whose nature is unknown.
:::

## Six numbers

The simplest ΛCDM model has just six free parameters. One common choice:

1. $\Omega_{\mathrm{b}} h^2$: density of ordinary matter;
2. $\Omega_{\mathrm{c}} h^2$: density of cold dark matter;
3. $\theta_*$: the angular size of the sound horizon in the CMB (closely related
   to $H_0$);
4. $\tau$: how much the CMB light was scattered after the first stars reionised
   the universe;
5. $A_{\mathrm{s}}$: the strength of the initial fluctuations;
6. $n_{\mathrm{s}}$: how that strength changes with scale.

Here $h = H_0 / (100\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})$. Everything else, including
$H_0$, the age and $\Omega_\Lambda$, is derived from these six.

:::note Planck 2018 values
$H_0 = 67.4 \pm 0.5$ km/s/Mpc, $\Omega_{\mathrm{m}} = 0.315 \pm 0.007$,
$n_{\mathrm{s}} = 0.965 \pm 0.004$, age $= 13.80 \pm 0.02$ billion years.
The Cosmos app uses the closely related "Planck 2018" parameter set that includes
baryon acoustic oscillation data ($H_0 = 67.66$, $\Omega_{\mathrm{m}} = 0.3097$).
:::

## Computing the age of the universe

Since $H = \dot{a}/a$, a small time step is $dt = da/(aH)$. Adding up all steps
from the Big Bang ($a = 0$) to today ($a = 1$):

$$t_0 = \int_0^1 \frac{da}{a\,H(a)} = \frac{1}{H_0}\int_0^1 \frac{da}{\sqrt{\Omega_{\mathrm{r},0}a^{-2} + \Omega_{\mathrm{m},0}a^{-1} + \Omega_{k,0} + \Omega_{\Lambda,0}\,a^{2}}}$$

The integral has to be evaluated numerically for our universe. For the Planck
2018 parameters it gives $t_0 \approx 0.955/H_0 \approx 13.8$ billion years.

:::example Coincidence explained
The age is very close to the Hubble time $1/H_0$. Deceleration in the matter era
(which makes the universe younger than $1/H_0$) and acceleration in the dark
energy era (which makes it older) almost cancel. This is a coincidence of our
particular moment in cosmic history.
:::

## Successes of ΛCDM

The same six numbers explain, among other things:

- the detailed pattern of temperature fluctuations in the CMB;
- the abundances of hydrogen, helium and deuterium from nucleosynthesis;
- the distribution of galaxies and baryon acoustic oscillations;
- the Hubble diagram of Type Ia supernovae;
- gravitational lensing by large-scale structure.

## Cracks in the model?

ΛCDM is a working description, not a final theory:

- the physical nature of dark matter and dark energy is unknown;
- local measurements of $H_0$ (about 73) disagree with the value inferred from the
  CMB (about 67): the **Hubble tension**;
- the clumpiness of matter measured by lensing surveys is slightly lower than
  predicted (the *S8 tension*);
- recent survey results hint that dark energy might evolve.

Whether these are measurement issues or signs of new physics is one of the most
exciting questions in cosmology today.

## Summary

- ΛCDM: flat universe with a cosmological constant, cold dark matter, baryons and
  radiation.
- Today: about 69% dark energy, 26% dark matter, 5% ordinary matter.
- Six parameters fit a wide range of observations; $H_0 \approx 67$–$68$ km/s/Mpc
  and $t_0 \approx 13.8$ Gyr.
- The age follows from $t_0 = \int_0^1 da/(aH)$.
- Open tensions may point to new physics.
